These projects are where the fundamentals came from. AIE Melbourne gave me the groundwork - engine architecture, maths, and how a game is actually structured - and I taught myself an enormous amount on top of it by building these in my own time. A lot of the early gameplay work was Unity and C#, which is where I learned how gameplay and engine tooling fit together before I started writing my own. SlimeCube was the turning point: putting a mesh and PBR pipeline together in OpenGL showed me how much deeper rendering goes, and it grew directly into the Vulkan SlimeProject. These days I prototype in my own C++ engines instead, and that jump is the throughline of everything I have built since.
